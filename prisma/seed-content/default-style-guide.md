# LinkedIn Repost Captions & Comments: Writing Instructions

## Purpose

Create LinkedIn repost captions and comments that feel like a genuine
human response to the original post, rather than a summary, promotion,
or generic engagement statement.

The writing must preserve the meaning and intent of the original post
while adding a clear, focused perspective.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Core Principle

Every repost or comment must be built around **one single takeaway**
from the original post.

Do not try to cover multiple points from the source. Identify the
strongest or most relevant idea and build the entire response around it.

The result should communicate:

> **One idea. One message. One connected storyline.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Single Takeaway

-   Select **one** meaningful takeaway from the original post.
-   Do not summarize the entire post.
-   Do not introduce secondary ideas simply because they appear in the
    source.
-   The chosen takeaway should be specific enough to give the response a
    clear point of view.
-   Everything in the caption or comment must reinforce this takeaway.

### Test

If the response can be reduced to two or three unrelated messages, it is
too broad.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Singular Strong Message

The response must have **one strong central message**.

The reader should understand what the writer wants them to think about
by the end.

Avoid: - Multiple conclusions - Lists of observations - Generic praise
followed by unrelated commentary - Several disconnected industry
points - A summary of everything the original post said

The message should be strong enough to stand on its own, while remaining
faithful to the source.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Integrated Storyline

The writing must read as **one continuous thought**.

Every sentence must naturally follow from the sentence before it.

Use this progression where appropriate:

**Opening observation → explanation → implication → concluding thought**

The connection between sentences should feel logical rather than
mechanical.

### Avoid disjointed writing

Do not write:

> AI is changing payments.\
> Trust is becoming more important.\
> Banks need better infrastructure.\
> The future will be interesting.

These are separate statements, not a storyline.

Instead, build a chain of thought in which each sentence creates the
reason for the next sentence.

### Sentence-flow test

After writing each sentence, ask:

**"Why does the next sentence come immediately after this one?"**

If the answer is unclear, rewrite the transition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Strict Adherence to the Original Post

The original post is the **source of truth**.

The repost caption or comment must strictly adhere to its: - Facts -
Arguments - Examples - Context - Claims - Conclusions - People and
organizations mentioned - Specific themes and terminology

Do not: - Add facts that are not supported by the original post - Invent
statistics - Attribute opinions that were not expressed - Introduce
unrelated industry trends - Change the meaning of the original
argument - Overstate what the original post says - Use the source merely
as a jumping-off point for an unrelated opinion

A response can add perspective, but that perspective must remain
directly connected to the original content.

### Accuracy test

Before publishing, ask:

**"Can every substantive claim in this response be traced directly to
the original post?"**

If not, remove or revise it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Language Level

Use **high-school-level English**.

The writing should be: - Clear - Direct - Natural - Easy to understand -
Professional without sounding formal - Conversational without becoming
casual

Prefer simple words over unnecessarily sophisticated vocabulary.

### Prefer

-   "The real shift is..."
-   "That matters because..."
-   "This changes how..."
-   "The point is simple..."
-   "What stands out is..."

### Avoid

-   Excessive jargon
-   Academic language
-   Corporate clichés
-   Over-engineered phrasing
-   Long, complicated sentence structures
-   Language that sounds generated or scripted

The goal is **executive clarity**, not linguistic complexity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Highly Personalised and Conversational

The response should sound like a person who actually read the original
post and has a reason for sharing or responding to it.

It should feel: - Personal - Specific - Conversational - Thoughtful -
Human

Avoid generic phrases that could accompany almost any LinkedIn post.

### Avoid generic openings

-   "Great insights."
-   "This is a very important topic."
-   "Couldn't agree more."
-   "Well said."
-   "Interesting perspective."
-   "The future is here."

These add little value unless they are followed immediately by a
specific, source-grounded thought.

### Better approach

Start with the specific idea that made the post worth sharing or
commenting on.

The reader should be able to tell that the writer engaged with the
actual post.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Repost Caption Length

**Target: 80 words.**

The repost caption should be concise enough to preserve the original
post's importance while providing a distinct reason to engage with it.

Do not pad the caption to reach the word count.

A strong 72--85-word caption is preferable to an 80-word caption filled
with repetition.

### Repost structure

A useful default structure is:

1.  **Strong opening:** Identify the one idea that stands out.
2.  **Development:** Explain why that idea matters.
3.  **Implication:** Connect the idea to the audience or broader
    context, only where supported by the original post.
4.  **Close:** Leave the reader with a clear takeaway or reason to
    consider the post.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Comment Length

**Target: 45 words.**

A comment should be tighter than a repost because the original post is
already visible immediately above it.

Do not repeat the original post.

Instead: - Pick one specific idea. - React to it. - Add a concise
observation grounded in the post. - End with a clear thought where
appropriate.

A comment should feel like a genuine response in a conversation, not a
miniature repost.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Opening Hook

The first sentence matters.

Open with the **specific insight**, not with empty praise.

The hook can: - State the key takeaway - Highlight a tension - Point to
an important implication - Identify what is easy to miss - Express a
clear personal reaction

The hook should create a reason to read the next sentence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Sentence-to-Sentence Continuity

This is a strict requirement.

**Every sentence must follow the previous sentence.**

Avoid writing a collection of good sentences that do not form a coherent
narrative.

Use logical relationships such as: - Because → therefore - Observation →
implication - Problem → consequence - Change → response - Claim → why it
matters - Example → lesson

Transitions do not always need explicit linking words. The logic itself
should create the flow.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. No Unnecessary Summary

A repost caption is **not a summary of the original post**.

Do not write:

> "This post discusses X, Y and Z. It also highlights A and B. These are
> important because..."

Instead, identify the single idea that deserves amplification and build
around it.

The reader should get the essence of the response without feeling that
the original post has been rewritten.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Audience Relevance

Keep the audience in mind, particularly when the original post is aimed
at: - Financial institution executives - Banking leaders - Fintech
executives - Technology leaders - Enterprise decision-makers - Industry
stakeholders

The writing should respect an executive reader's time.

Lead with relevance and outcome rather than background explanation.

Do not become salesy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Brand and Executive Voice

When writing on behalf of a company:

-   Sound confident and informed.
-   Keep the focus on the idea rather than the brand.
-   Avoid promotional language unless the original post specifically
    calls for it.
-   Do not turn a repost into a product pitch.
-   Let the relevance of the insight create the value.

When writing for an executive:

-   Make it personal.
-   Use a clear point of view.
-   Avoid sounding like corporate communications.
-   Do not over-polish the language to the point that it loses
    personality.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. CTA Rules

A repost does not automatically need a "Read the post" CTA.

The close should serve the storyline.

Use a CTA only when it feels natural and relevant to the original
content.

Prefer: - A question that extends the thought - A clear takeaway - A
reason to consider the original post - A direct invitation to engage
with the idea

Avoid generic CTAs such as: - "Read more." - "Check it out." - "Click
the link." - "Let us know your thoughts."

unless the context genuinely requires one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Hashtags

Use hashtags only when appropriate to the platform context and original
post.

When hashtags are required for a branded repost, use: - **1 branded
hashtag** - **1--2 niche hashtags** - **1--2 industry hashtags**

Keep the hashtag set relevant to the actual content.

Do not use hashtags simply to fill space.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. What to Avoid

Do not use: - Disjointed sentences - Generic praise - Empty corporate
language - Excessive jargon - Unrelated opinions - Unsupported claims -
Invented facts - Overly promotional language - Repetition of the
original post - Multiple takeaways - Multiple competing messages -
Artificially dramatic language - Filler sentences - Excessive hashtags -
Emojis when writing formal B2B LinkedIn content - Em dashes - "Not
only... but also..." constructions - Phrases that make the writing sound
obviously AI-generated

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Final Quality Checklist

Before delivering any repost caption or comment, verify:

### Content

-   [ ] Is there exactly **one takeaway**?
-   [ ] Is there exactly **one strong message**?
-   [ ] Does every sentence reinforce that message?
-   [ ] Does the response strictly adhere to the original post?
-   [ ] Are there any unsupported facts or claims?
-   [ ] Have unrelated ideas been removed?

### Flow

-   [ ] Does the opening create a reason to continue reading?
-   [ ] Does every sentence naturally follow the previous one?
-   [ ] Does the response read as one integrated storyline?
-   [ ] Is there a clear progression from thought to thought?
-   [ ] Does the ending complete the idea?

### Voice

-   [ ] Is the English at a high-school reading level?
-   [ ] Does it sound human and conversational?
-   [ ] Is it genuinely personalised to the original post?
-   [ ] Does it avoid generic LinkedIn language?
-   [ ] Does it sound confident without being salesy?

### Length

-   [ ] Repost: approximately **80 words**
-   [ ] Comment: approximately **45 words**

### Final test

Read the response once without looking at the original post.

Then ask:

**"What is the one thing this response wants me to take away?"**

If the answer is not immediate, rewrite it.

Then read it against the original post and ask:

**"Is every substantive point here faithful to what the original post
actually says?"**

If not, rewrite it.
